1. Direct Button Contact

Pressing a candidate button closes a mechanical contact switch on the Ballot Unit. Hardware debouncing filters noise to prevent false or duplicate triggering.

2. Isolated Cable Signal

The Ballot Unit encodes the pressed candidate key index (1–8) and transmits a simple serialized digital packet across the isolated interconnect cable.

3. Controller Verification & Latch

The Control Unit processor checks if the machine is currently in the VOTING ENABLED state. If valid, it increments the candidate's register in secure memory.

4. Instant Hardware Lockout

The controller immediately triggers the piezo confirmation tone, lights the selected candidate's LED, and locks the machine. Any subsequent button presses are ignored until the operator reactivates it.

* Conceptual visualization for educational purposes. Real voting systems utilize custom sealed microcontrollers with masked ROM and cryptographic verification.

HOW DOES THE EVM WORK?

Follow the operational cycle of electronic voting from authorization to lockout

STEP 1
Operator Enables Voting
The polling officer presses ENABLE NEXT VOTE on the Control Unit. The machine leaves the locked state and sets the READY status.
STEP 2
One Voter Presses One Button
The voter enters the compartment and presses the blue rectangular button for their chosen candidate. All buttons are now active.
STEP 3
Selection Recorded
The candidate vote is registered in secure memory. The total vote counter increases by one.
STEP 4
Confirmation Displayed
A short confirmation beep sounds, the candidate's red LED lights up, and the VVPAT paper slip verifies the selection.
STEP 5
Machine Locks
All candidate buttons are immediately disabled. Pressing other buttons has zero effect. Duplicate voting is impossible.
STEP 6
Operator Enables Next Vote
The next registered voter is authenticated in the queue before the officer pushes the button to begin the cycle anew.
